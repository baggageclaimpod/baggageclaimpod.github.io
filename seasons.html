Seasons We've Covered

Baggage Claim launched with Schengen Showdown, and we have covered every season from then onwards. During the offseasons, we work our way through the backlog. This page contains a brief description of each season, a link to the first episode of our coverage, and the bingo boards we create before each one.

Season 1: Connect 4 Across America

Our coverage of Connect 4 begins with our Episode 9. Considering that this was the first-ever season of Jet Lag: The Game, it was a lot of fun to come back to it after a few years. This was back when Ben and Adam were “not famous enough to talk,” teams had to be legally required to eat, game days started at a grueling 4am, and poor Brian from RealEngineering was barely briefed on the game design before starting play. We talk a lot about defense vs. offsense in our strat chats for this season, and boil simulations down to their barest bones. Despite only requiring tic-tac-toe logic, this season immediately cemented itself as a classic.

Season 2: Circumnavigation

Coverage of Circumnavigation begins in Episode 21. Perhaps one of the most polarizing seasons of Jet Lag, Season 2 consists of Ben and Adam are racing against Sam and Joseph from RealLifeLore to circumnavigate the globe in 100 hours. While revisitingi this season, we realize exactly how many iconic moments that have been cemented in JLTG lore came from Circumnavigation and the soft spot in our hearts for early JLTG seasons grows even softer. In our discussions, we try to create our own optimal Circumnavigation routes and discover that while Jaya absolutely LOVES coming up with complicated flight paths, it makes Casey feel like she's dying. Jaya also gives Casey an existential crisis during a game about high-altitude birds, and we all get REALLY hungry for noodles in Singapore.

Season 3: Tag Eur It 1

Coverage of Tag 1 begins in Episode 36. Our coverage was split between two offseasons, so it was completed in Episodes 49 and 50. The first episode of Tag 1 remains one of the most-watched videos from Jet Lag: The Game, and in rewatching this season, we understand exactly why. Not only do an absolute barrage of iconic moments come from the first couple episodes on their own, but it was also the first round robin season -- and therefore our first time seeing how each player fares on their own. While unpacking Tag 1, we find out that Kyra is terrible at estimating big numbers, we declare our solidarity with Sam's controversial assessment that humans ARE animals, and Jaya tries to kill Casey and Kyra with Tuberculosis.

Season 4: Battle 4 America

Coverage of Battle 4 America begins in our Episode 62. Our coverage was split between two offseasons, so it was completed with Episodes 72 and 73. Battle 4 America was the first season that all three of us watched, so we came back to it with love in our hearts and it welcomed us in with open arms. In this season, Brian from RealEngineering is back to pair up with Sam and battle Badam for ownership of US states. While discussing Battle 4 America, we talk about Buc-ees at length, declare our undying loyalty to the DC Metro, and declare that soup in a helicopter is one of our favorite challenges of all time.

Season 5: Race to the End of the World

Coverage of New Zealand begins in our Episode 84. It's the first season with Toby Hendy from Tibeez, who has become a (very deserving) fan favorite guest on Jet Lag: The Game! She and Sam are racing Ben and Adam in a road trip from the northernmost point in New Zealand to the southernmost, using the NZ highway system as one enormous board game. I will update this description when we finish coverage :)

Season 13: Schengen Showdown

Coverage of Schengen Showdown begins with our Episode 1! Schengen Showdown is the season that made Casey say "Wait a minute... Why doesn't Jet Lag have a companion podcast? Hey guys.... What if we started it?" so we feel very fondly about it. This was Tom Scott's first appearance on Jet Lag, a crossover event that made Jaya go absolutely feral. As a large-scale claimer-style game, it harkens back to Battle 4 America as Sam and Tom race Ben and Adam to visit and claim as many countries in the Schengen Zone as possible. We find our podcasting sea legs while covering this season, starting segments that remain recurring and beloved over a year later. In these segments, we talk about Deutsche Bahn, learn about niche museums, find out that Ode to Joy is playing in Jaya's mind at all times (and they thought that was normal), and accidentally predict a handful of challenges.

Season 14: SnaKe

Our coverage of SnaKe South Korea begins with our predictions in Episode 13. SnaKe is a round robin-style game where Ben, Adam, and Sam take turns trying to build the longest snaking chain of South Korean train stations while the other two try their hardest to force a crash. This was one of the most brainy and and crunchy complicated games so far, and viewers were polarized. This meant that we had a ton of fun in our Strat Chats for the season, though, because we had TONS to talk about. These topics included: different ways to force a snaker to crash, the validity of dead end train lines, the importance of patience despite wanting to avoid stalemates, and extensive discussions about high versus low-speed trains. Jaya also makes Casey and Kyra learn about ancient games, we hear a lot about the impeachment of Yoon Suk Yeol, and Casey finally gets a new style of game to work.

Season 15: Tag Eur It: All-Stars

Our coverage of Tag All-Stars begins with our predictions in Episode 27. The Jet Lag crew is playing Tag across central Europe for a fourth time, but this time they've brought back the all-stars: Toby Hendy, Brian McManus, and Michelle Khare are paired up with Sam, Ben, and Adam! Having three teams suddenly gives the psychological warfare of this show so much more sauce, and we have an absolute blast unpacking it. Casey has us figure out the cost differences between flights and trains after Ben and Brian get rid of their grappling hooks, Jet Lag passes the Bechdel Test, Jaya finally gets to infodump about a really weird Swiss astronomer (but fails to create a rail electrification game that took another two seasons to happen), and Kyra makes some insane predictions.
